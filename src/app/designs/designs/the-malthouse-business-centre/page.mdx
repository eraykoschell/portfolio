# The Malthouse Business Centre

<ExternalLink url="https://themalthousebusinesscentre.co.uk/" label="The Malthouse Business Centre"/>, a family-run business, is situated in the heart of Ormskirk, Lancashire. Purchased in 1994 by the Lloyd family, this historic 19th-century building – once used for drying grain to make malt – has been lovingly renovated over the past two decades. Today, it offers high-quality office spaces and business units in a unique, characterful setting.

<Image src={PhysicalBusiness} alt="The Malthouse Business Centre building exterior" className={styles.thumbnail}/>

## Motivation

I completed this project during my time at <ExternalLink url="https://www.edgehill.ac.uk/" label="Edge Hill University"/>, where we were asked to pick a local business and redesign its digital experience around user needs, business goals, and usability.

I chose this brief because it reflects real agency and product work: identifying a client opportunity, analysing an existing interface, and proposing practical improvements grounded in UX principles. The goal was to improve the online presence of <ExternalLink url="https://themalthousebusinesscentre.co.uk/" label="The Malthouse Business Centre"/>, making it easier for potential tenants to find information and get in touch.

## Method and scope

I used the <ExternalLink url="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/" label="NN/g: 10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design"/> to review the existing site and produce solution-driven redesign ideas.

Why this method:
- It is effective for spotting usability issues early in the design process.
- It does not require recruiting users, which made it realistic for a student timeline and local-business budget.
- It gave me a structured way to connect design decisions to clear usability problems.

Scope of evaluation:
- User group: business managers/directors looking for office or unit space.
- Primary goals: help visitors understand the offer quickly and drive enquiries through contact form or phone.
- Platforms: desktop and mobile journeys for browsing, comparing spaces, and contacting the business.

## Discovery

I researched the business and its target audience, then reviewed the existing website to identify opportunities for improvement.

Key findings included:
- The text contrast was poor, making it difficult to read, especially with the slideshow images.
- There was no clear call to action, making it hard to determine the next steps.
- The logo is bulky and not designed with mobile screens in mind.
- It isn't clear what the offering is as the information was buried in the text.

Existing strengths worth preserving:
- Strong social proof with a dedicated tenants page.
- Bright imagery that helps users visualise the spaces.
- Contact details in the footer for quick access.

Heuristic issues identified:
- Aesthetic and minimalist design
- Visibility of system status
- Match between the system and the real world
- Consistency and standards
- Recognition rather than recall
- Help and documentation
- Help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors

## Design

My scope was to keep the design simple and clean, prioritising the key information potential tenants need.

I designed for both desktop and mobile and avoided straying too far from the original visual direction to preserve brand recognition.

I worked iteratively, creating components and visual styles as needed (colour, typography, layout blocks), then linking them into two prototype flows: one desktop and one mobile.

### Colours

I used Tailwind's super helpful [Colours](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/colors) documentation to pick out stronger colours that delivered better contrast and readability. I used the [Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) to ensure that the colours met accessibility standards.

<Image src={BrandColours} alt="Brand Colours" />

### Typography

I chose a serif font to give a sense of tradition and history, which is fitting for a business located in a historic building.

It also aligns with the seriousness of the business, which is important for attracting potential tenants.

<Image src={BrandTypography} alt="Brand Typography" />


### Logo

The original logo resembles the physical building of the business, however, it is not designed with mobile screens in mind. It was hard to fit into the header without taking up too much space or looking awkward.

<Image src={OriginalLogo} alt="Original Logo" style={{ maxWidth: "400px" }} />

I redesigned the logo to be more compact and versatile, while still maintaining the essence of the original design, mainly by simplifying the building illustration.

<Image src={NewLogo} alt="New Logo" style={{ maxWidth: "400px" }} />

## Pages

Overall, my aim was to give all the elements of the page more breathing room, making it easier for users to find the information they need and navigate the site.

### Home

There was no clear call to action on the original home page. Based on business goals, I prioritised two primary user actions: browsing available spaces and viewing current tenants.

## Reflection

This project helped me practise balancing business goals with user needs under realistic constraints. The heuristic method was useful for producing clear, actionable design changes quickly.

If this were moving beyond concept stage, my next step would be usability testing with prospective tenants to validate the redesigned journeys and measure whether enquiry actions improved.
